# Exploration activity: Evidence or imagination?

A self-paced activity built around the Gokstad Ship Explorer. It is written for any history or humanities course and needs no particular textbook. Students need a computer, tablet or phone with a current browser. Plan on 45 to 60 minutes in total.

Link: https://wangy113.github.io/early-viking-simulation/

## Learning objective

After this activity, students will be able to tell apart what is known from a find, what is known from other evidence, and what is a reconstruction, and explain why the difference matters when we look at images of the past.

Supporting objectives:

1. Describe three features of the Gokstad ship and what each one tells us about how it was built or used.
2. Classify a claim about the past as found, other evidence, or reconstruction, and give a reason.
3. Explain how a realistic picture can make a guess look like a fact.

## Before the visit (10 minutes)

Read the welcome screen and the About and sources page in the app. Then answer in two or three sentences:

> Where does our knowledge of the Gokstad ship come from? Name two kinds of evidence.

## During the visit (25 to 35 minutes)

Visit all 12 stops. Use Guide me, the All stops list, or the numbered markers. If the 3D scene does not work for you, use the Text version. It has all the same content.

Then complete these four tasks.

**1. Found with this ship.** Choose one stop tagged *Found with this ship*. What was found, and what does it tell us?

**2. Known from other finds.** Choose one stop tagged *Known from other finds*. What do we know from other sites, and why can we not be sure it applies to this ship?

**3. Reconstruction.** Choose one stop tagged *Reconstruction*. What part is a guess, and what evidence would settle the question?

**4. Look past the labels.** Find one thing in the scene that has no stop of its own, for example the people's faces, the beach camp or the hills. Is it evidence or imagination? How can you tell?

## After the visit (10 to 15 minutes)

Discussion prompt, for a discussion board or a short written response:

> The scene looks realistic, but much of it is reconstruction. Is a realistic picture more helpful or more misleading than a simple drawing for learning about the past? Use at least two stops as examples.

## Check your understanding

Five optional quiz questions. Answers are at the end.

1. How do historians know the Gokstad ship was built around 890?
   a. A date carved on the stem
   b. Tree rings in its timber
   c. A saga that describes the ship
   d. Coins found in the hull

2. The ship had 16 oar ports on each side. Where were they?
   a. In the top plank, the rail
   b. In the third plank down from the top
   c. In the bottom plank, near the keel
   d. In the deck boards

3. In the app, the shields hang only along the back half of the ship. Why?
   a. The Gokstad ship had shields on the back half only
   b. Shields were only allowed near the steersman
   c. It is a teaching choice, so you can compare covered and open oar ports
   d. The front shields were lost before the excavation

4. Which of these is tagged *Reconstruction* in the app?
   a. The 16 planks on each side
   b. The rudder on the right side
   c. Rowers sitting on sea chests
   d. The horn gaming pieces

5. The word *starboard* comes from:
   a. The star-shaped boards on the bow
   b. Steering by the stars
   c. The steering board on the right side of the ship
   d. The sternpost

## Accessibility and access

- Every stop's content is in the Text version, so students who cannot use the 3D scene can complete every task.
- Pause motion stops the moving people, sea and smoke.
- The app collects no student data. Students submit their answers through your course site.

## Answers

1. b. 2. b. 3. c. 4. c. 5. c.
