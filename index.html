Text version of the scene

The Gokstad ship rests on log rollers on a sandy beach, its bow pointing toward a fjord with mountains beyond. It is about 23 meters long and 5 meters wide, built of overlapping oak planks, with a tall curved post at each end, a mast amidships and a row of yellow and black shields along the rail. Crew members work on and around it. On the beach to the left of the ship is a small camp with a tent, a cooking fire, two people playing a board game, horses and a dog. A small boat lies on the sand near the water.

About the Gokstad ship

In 1880, a team led by the archaeologist Nicolay Nicolaysen dug into a large burial mound at Gokstad farm near Sandefjord, Norway. Inside lay a ship about 23 meters long and 5 meters wide. Tree rings in its timber show it was built around 890. A few years later a man was laid in a timber burial chamber on board, and the ship was covered with earth.

With him were many grave goods, including a gaming board, kitchen equipment, a tent, a sledge, beds, three small boats and the bones of horses, dogs and birds. The mound had been robbed long ago, which may explain why few weapons were found.

In 1893 a full-size copy named Viking sailed from Bergen across the Atlantic in 27 days and on to the World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago, showing how seaworthy the design was. The original ship is preserved in Oslo.

What is real in this scene

The ship's shape, size, planks, oar ports, shields, rudder and mast follow the Gokstad ship. The sail's size and pattern are a reconstruction. The people, their clothes and their tasks are illustrations drawn from Viking Age evidence, not portraits of real individuals.
